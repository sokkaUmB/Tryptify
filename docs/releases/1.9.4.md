# Tryptify 1.9.4

Your own music as a galaxy: every artist on your phone a star beside their genre, their albums planets and their tracks moons, and it works offline. Both galaxies get search, a stronger zoom and lighter controls. Lyrics find songs named by number and songs on compilations, the player's panels read in light themes, and exclusive mode drives more USB DACs, Apple's USB-C headphone adapter among them, at their full output.

Each change is marked New, Changed, Removed or Fixed.

- New: **Your library as a galaxy.** Library galaxy, under Folders in Library, shows every artist on your phone as a star beside their genre on the genre map. Artists whose genre isn't recognised float above and below the disc.
- New: **Albums in orbit.** An artist's first album orbits nearest and each later one further out; albums you play a lot glow brighter. After eight albums the rest form an asteroid belt, and an album's extra tracks a ring. Tap a moon to play it, long-press a star to list every album and track.
- New: **Works offline.** Everything on it comes from the music and play history on your phone.
- Changed: **Short tracks in your library.** A library scan no longer leaves out tracks under 30 seconds, so intros, interludes and skits are in your albums. Atmos music videos of any length are found too.
- New: **Choose a song row's badges.** Settings › Appearance › Song row badges turns off the source (Local, Qobuz, TIDAL), quality, format, download and length marks on every song list, one by one or all at once, so a song's artist and album get the whole line.
- New: **Track info.** A song's menu (long-press, or its ⋮) has Track info: its file's format, sample rate, bit depth, bitrate, length and size, and every tag in it by name, ReplayGain and MusicBrainz included, ready to select and copy. A streamed song shows its download or cached copy, or else what the app knows of it. The menu itself is glass now, over the list it came from.

## Discover

- New: **Search the galaxy.** A search button on both galaxies finds a genre, by any of its names ("dnb" finds drum and bass), or an artist, and flies there.
- New: **One sky, two galaxies.** Your library galaxy sits in the genre galaxy's sky. Switch between them from the title bar and the view stays where it was.
- Changed: **Galaxy and Timeline are two pills.** The panel at the bottom of the map is two glass pills now. Surprise me is in the title bar, and how much you have explored is under the title.
- Changed: **A stronger zoom.** A pinch zooms further, starts at once, and stays on the planet you are zooming toward. Tapping a planet or a star brings it closer.

## Now playing

- Changed: **Long titles show in full.** A song title too long for the player slides along to show the rest of itself, then back to its start, instead of ending in "…".
- Changed: **Glass Visualizer and Waveform buttons**, like Timer, Sound, Speed and Mixer above them.
- Fixed: **BPM at the right tempo.** The Speed panel's BPM read hardcore and DnB at half and some slow songs at double, and stopped at 200. It now takes the fastest count whose beats nearly all hold a kick or a snare, however loud each one is in the mix, up to 250, and settles on what most of its readings agree on rather than the first. A song on your phone with a BPM tag shows its tag at once; a tap on the number still measures it from the audio. One known miss: a DnB two-step whose snare sits far under its kick can still read at half.
- Fixed: **Panels read in light themes.** With dynamic colour in a light theme, the Speed panel, the audio tools and the preset browser had dark text on dark glass and lost their outlines. Their text is light and their pills and switches show.

## Lyrics

- Fixed: **Songs named by number.** Untagged songs named like "2-05 Song" or "CD1 - 05 - Song" are searched without the number, and no longer get "01" or "CD1" as their artist. Refresh the library once to fix songs already scanned.
- Fixed: **Compilations.** Songs on a Various Artists album are searched under their own artist.
- Fixed: **The right lyrics for your own files.** Songs on your phone are no longer looked up on TIDAL by a made-up number, which could bring back another song's lyrics.

## Exclusive USB DAC

- Changed: **More DACs in exclusive mode.** DACs in USB Audio 3.0's basic mode describe no formats of their own, and exclusive mode used to give up on them. Apple's USB-C to 3.5 mm adapter is one, because Android always puts it in that mode. They now play at 48 kHz, in 24-bit.
- Changed: **DACs at their full output.** The DAC's own volume is unmuted and set to full, never past 0 dB, whenever a song starts, as USB Audio Player Pro does. Android's volume never reaches a DAC in exclusive mode, so a DAC left turned down stayed down. The player's volume bar is the only volume now, and still starts at −24 dB. The DAC card shows the result, as HW vol 0 dB.
- Fixed: **DACs that refused every song.** Some DACs were turned away with "no alt setting matches" whatever played. Those whose packets are sized exactly to their rate, Apple's adapter included, play now. A song at a rate the DAC can't take, such as 44.1 kHz on a basic-mode DAC, says so and plays through Android instead.
- Changed: **The DAC card shows the DAC's release**, as rev, where it used to be labelled as the USB version.
- Removed: **USB DAC bit-perfect routing.** It pointed Android's output at a USB DAC, still through Android's own mixer. Exclusive USB DAC is the bit-perfect path, so it is the one switch now; the first-run setup says where to find it.
